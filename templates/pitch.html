5. Advanced Interpretation: The "Achilles' Heel"

A user might discover that a dataset with both positive and negative numbers (like 15, -5, 2, 12, -8) results in a Predictability Score of 0%. This is not a bug; it is the tool's most important warning sign.

Why does this happen? Our score is based on a formula that measures how "spread out" the data is relative to its average. When a dataset contains both positive and negative values, the average can get very close to zero. Dividing the large "spread" by a near-zero average results in an extremely high volatility metric, which correctly drives the Predictability Score to its floor: 0%.

What it means for the user: A 0% score in this scenario is the tool's way of screaming a warning: "This dataset is so volatile that it crosses the zero-line. The average is almost meaningless as a predictor, and the concept of 'predictability' itself is breaking down." It has correctly identified a dataset whose character is fundamentally unstable and high-risk.
